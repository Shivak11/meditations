# Organise the reflection by topic

Make the main headline a direct sentence summarising what was reviewed or clarified. A bare list of topic names does not provide that recap. Name additional topics in one short sentence below when needed.

Read this before drafting any meditation. The purpose is to make a useful mental model, principle or rule understandable and easy to recall. The topics come from the work reviewed; they need not share a theme.

## Reader sequence

Begin with a direct account of the session or work. For example: “This session, you clarified how a time adjustment should change your calendar.” Add one short sentence for other topics only when there are other topics. Compact topic links can help a longer study. The opening belongs with the first topic, without a separate cover.

For each actual topic:

1. Name what was discussed in plain language.
2. State the useful principle immediately in one clear sentence, with visual emphasis. A tentative idea can start with “A possibility to test is…” when the evidence warrants only a proposal.
3. Briefly recall the specific question, observation or correction that produced it. Use “you” only when the supplied conversation supports that account. Distinguish an assistant suggestion from the user's finding.
4. Show one concrete application: an example, comparison, table, diagram or small interaction that makes the consequence apparent.

These are content roles, not four compulsory labels to print on every page. Use connected paragraphs and meaningful headings. Do not display the five-R phases or an evidence/confidence/record-verdict form.

## Length and evidence

Keep each concept within one readable A4 page, including the opening on the first page. A shorter treatment is welcome. Do not force a topic count or divide one concept to claim compliance. Preserve natural scrolling on a phone. Edit excess content instead of shrinking the type or hiding the explanation.

Keep the account of how the principle emerged factual. Do not invent a discovery story, established framework, psychological explanation or measured improvement. A useful idea may remain a proposal. Include a qualification once where it changes interpretation; keep source inventories and operational notes outside the HTML.

Examine alternative explanations internally. Show a limit, counterexample or next experiment only when it adds something specific to this principle. A complete example needs no compulsory quiz, slider, extra panel or final action list.

## Neutral examples of the sequence

These examples illustrate writing structure; they are not mandatory subjects or historical user sessions.

### Adjusting a calendar event

**Keep the start time fixed when extending an event.**

You asked to add 15 minutes to a 9:00–10:00 block. The first revision moved it to 9:15–10:15. You clarified that you wanted more time, so the start stayed at 9:00 and the end moved to 10:15.

| Change | Time | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Move the whole event | 9:15–10:15 | 60 minutes |
| Extend the event | 9:00–10:15 | 75 minutes |

The two changes affect different values. This explains the requested correction without a separate test question.

### A tentative notification change

**Muting notifications may help you complete a writing block with fewer interruptions.**

You described two interrupted writing blocks and one quiet block in which you finished a paragraph. That suggests a setting worth trying; it does not show that the setting caused the difference.

A short comparison can keep the claim specific: use the same-length writing block with notifications muted and record the interruptions. The result would inform this proposal, without establishing a rule for every task.

## Language check

Read every visible sentence as plain text before reviewing layout, including chart labels, controls, accessible descriptions, revealed material and print alternatives. Use natural, literal sentences about actual inputs, actions, decisions and results. Explain an unfamiliar term with an example. Avoid metaphor, personification, dramatic language, slogans, fragment sequences, rhetorical “not X, but Y”, ornamental framework names, stacked labels and italics. Apply current explicit writing preferences without a routine reconfirmation question.

Design can vary with the subject. Preserve this information order and readability while changing colour, type, layout or diagram treatment. An approved example establishes the level of clarity; it does not require copying its topic or visual system.
